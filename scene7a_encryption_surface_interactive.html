The Inventory
WHERE DOES CRYPTO LIVE?
THE PICTURE
This grid is every place encryption touches an enterprise — from the laptop in someone's bag to the tape in a vault. Seven domains. Forty-two touchpoints. Every one of them has a cryptographic algorithm underneath.
THREE KINDS OF CRYPTO
Everything here does one of three jobs: moves data (in-transit), stores data (at-rest), or proves identity or integrity (signing). We'll walk through each — and show which ones a quantum computer breaks.
WHY A MAP MATTERS
You cannot migrate what you cannot see. Every PQC migration starts with this map.
>>
Category · In-Transit
DATA ON THE MOVE
WHAT BREAKS
Every secure channel — HTTPS, VPN, SSH, mTLS — begins with a handshake that uses ECDHE, X25519, or RSA key exchange to agree on a session key. A quantum computer watches the handshake and recovers the key. The session is readable.
WHY IT'S EVERYWHERE
Look at the grid — 11 of 42 touchpoints across nearly every domain rely on the same broken handshake. This is not one system you patch. It is the wiring of the internet.
THE HARVEST PROBLEM
HNDL (Harvest Now, Decrypt Later) — adversaries record encrypted traffic today and decrypt it later. Anything sent in-transit right now that needs secrecy past 2030 is already exposed.
THE FIX EXISTS
The hybrid cipher X25519+ML-KEM-768 (FIPS 203) replaces the key exchange. Google, Cloudflare, and Apple are already shipping it. The clock is inventory, not invention.

Category · At-Rest
DATA THAT SITS STILL
WHAT BREAKS
Disks, databases, backups, and object storage all use AES — which a quantum computer does not break. But the key that unlocks them is almost always wrapped with RSA. Break the wrapper, unlock the vault.
THE TAPE PROBLEM
12 of 42 touchpoints store data with RSA-wrapped keys. Backups and tape archives are the most exposed — they sit for years or decades. An attacker who copies a tape today — along with its RSA-wrapped key material — can decrypt it when a quantum computer arrives.
KMS AND HSM ARE NOT SAFE
Cloud KMS and HSM services protect the master keys — but those master keys are RSA or ECC. If the HSM speaks RSA, the HSM is in scope.
THE FIX
Re-wrap at-rest keys with ML-KEM. The AES underneath stays. The wrapper changes. Requires KMS/HSM vendor support — ask yours about their PQC roadmap.
ID
Category · Signatures & Identity
THE TRUST LAYER
WHAT BREAKS
Every digital signature — certificates, JWTs, code signatures, Git commits, passports — uses RSA, ECDSA, or Ed25519 (all discrete log problems, all broken by Shor's). A quantum computer forges them all. The trust chain that underpins the entire software supply chain collapses.
THE BIGGEST CATEGORY
19 of 42 touchpoints rely on signatures — the largest category. The entire Identity / PKI domain lights up. So does most of DevOps and the supply chain.
THIS IS HOW MALWARE BECOMES "LEGITIMATE"
Forge a code-signing certificate, sign your malware, ship it through the auto-update pipeline. Every endpoint installs it as a trusted update. A forged DNSSEC signature enables full DNS spoofing — even of HTTPS domains. These are the nightmare scenarios.
THE FIX
ML-DSA (FIPS 204) and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) replace RSA and ECDSA for signatures. PKI vendors are rolling out hybrid certs — ask your CA about their timeline.

The Playbook
WHAT LEADERSHIP OWNS
THE POSTURE
Every chip on the grid is now green — that is the destination. Getting there is a multi-year program, not a patch Tuesday. Here is the playbook.
01
Inventory (CBOM — Cryptographic Bill of Materials). Know where every RSA, ECDSA, and ECDHE lives. You cannot migrate what you cannot see.
02
Triage by horizon. Data with a secrecy lifetime past 2030 is harvest-now risk. Migrate those first.
03
Pressure your vendors. SaaS, CAs, HSM, KMS, CDN — if they don't have a PQC roadmap, you don't either.
04
Build crypto-agility. The goal isn't one swap — it's being able to swap algorithms again without a rewrite. Use abstraction layers (like TLS cipher negotiation) so swapping the underlying algorithm requires a config change, not a code rewrite.
05
Go hybrid first. Deploy classical + PQC together. Breaks nothing, buys time, protects against both worlds. Use X25519+ML-KEM-768 for key exchange and RSA+ML-DSA for signatures — both are shipping in major TLS libraries today.
06
Align to the deadlines. CNSA 2.0 wants PQC widespread by 2033. NIST and NSA set the tempo.
Encryption is not one project. It is 42 of them. Inventory first, migrate in waves. The clock started when the standards shipped.
